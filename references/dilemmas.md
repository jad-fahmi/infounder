# Dilemmas — the "when in doubt" answer key

Load this in `ask` mode when the user's doubt matches one of these. Each entry is a **default call** with the seats behind it (see `council.md`), the strongest dissent, and a test. These are starting points: adapt to the founder's stage, goal and repo; never paste them verbatim. Where the user's facts change the answer, say so.

Format: **Call** → **Why (seats)** → **Dissent** → **Test**.

---

## Idea & positioning

**1. "Everyone says my idea is taken / there are competitors."**
**Call:** Competition is proof of demand, not a verdict. Pick *one* wedge from the "it's taken" angles (`spark.md` §"It's taken") and be 10× better on that one dimension for one specific who. Kill it only if it's a tar pit and you have no new insight.
**Why:** Seibel (Dropbox was ~20th); Thiel (own a small market); Jobs (delete what sucks).
**Dissent:** Altman: if you're just the 1,001st clone, the first mover wins. You need an angle they can't copy by Tuesday.
**Test:** Finish "Unlike X, we ___ for ___ so they can ___." Show it to 10 target buyers. Pass = 3 ask for a demo or pre-order in 7 days.

**2. "ChatGPT / a big AI lab will kill my app."**
**Call:** If your value is a feature a model can ship in an update, assume it will. Move the moat to what a lab can't ship overnight: a community, proprietary data, distribution, a workflow you're embedded in, or the outcome you own. Ride model improvements instead of racing them.
**Why:** Altman (surf the wave; products that get better as models get better); Buffett/Munger (moat); Hormozi (vehicle: "true AI, not wrappers"); `ai-era.md`.
**Dissent:** Seibel: don't spiral; if customers pay you today, keep selling while you build the moat.
**Test (the Lab Test):** "If the lab shipped this tomorrow, would my customers still pay me, and why?" If the honest answer is "no", build the moat first.

**3. "Someone copied my product."**
**Call:** Being copied means you're the leader; copying means they're second. Don't panic-pivot or lawyer up. Ship faster, deepen the thing they can't see (the next step, the community, the data), and tell customers who you are.
**Why:** Altman (clones lose to the originator); Hormozi (copiers copy step five, not the reasoning that makes step six); Reilly (community).
**Dissent:** Zuckerberg: if the copy is better-distributed, speed alone won't save you, so own the relationship (list, community, integrations).
**Test:** Ship one meaningful release in 14 days; track whether paying customers churn. Pass = zero paid churn to the copy.

**3b. "Is my idea original enough / is it just an AI wrapper?"** → See `ai-era.md` (moat menu) and apply entry 2.

**4. "I have three ideas and can't choose."**
**Call:** Pick the one you can reach paying customers for fastest this month and burn the boats. Parallel ideas give you bad data and a third of your attention each.
**Why:** Hormozi (niche-slap; commitment is the elimination of alternatives); YC "pick one idea and go deep"; Altman (iterations).
**Dissent:** Thiel: pick the one with the best secret, not the quickest. Rule: if two are tied on reach, take the bigger secret.
**Test:** 48-hour test per idea (a landing page, 10 DMs, a pre-order link). Highest pre-orders wins; the others go in a drawer for 90 days.

**5. "My idea feels too small or too boring."**
**Call:** Small and boring are features: fewer entrants, faster first dollar. Start tiny and expand; boring categories have a higher hit rate than fun ones.
**Why:** Thiel (start small, monopolize); Zuckerberg (one campus at a time); Hormozi (uncool businesses, few entrants); Altman (step one before step two).
**Dissent:** Altman/Musk: if your goal is billions, the vehicle must allow it. Check "what does maxed-out look like?"
**Test:** Can you name the *next* adjacent customer after the first 100? If yes, the market isn't the constraint.

**6. "Should I build first or sell first?"**
**Call:** Sell first if you can fake the demo; build first only if a working version is cheaper than a demo (often true with AI). Either way, no multi-month build before real people have seen it.
**Why:** Maurya (demo → sell → build); Houston (a three-minute video *was* Dropbox's MVP); Seibel (turn the water on).
**Dissent:** Jobs: if the core experience is taste-dependent, a mock won't convey it; build the one thing well, small.
**Test:** A demo or prototype plus a pre-order or paid pilot link. Pass = 3 of 20 shown commit money or a calendar slot.

## Customers, product, growth

**7. "How do I get my first 10 customers?"**
**Call:** Customers 1-3 from your network; 4-10 by hand (warm outreach, in person, communities where the pain is public); no ads, no tools yet. → `launch.md`.
**Why:** Seibel/YC; Hormozi (warm outreach, 4 hours a day); Chesky (do things that don't scale); Collison (install it for them).
**Test:** 100 personalized messages in a week. Pass = 5 replies, 2 calls, 1 paid or pre-ordered.

**8. "Customers keep asking for features X, Y, Z."**
**Call:** Don't count requests; weigh *who* asks. Build what moves a paying customer to their main result; ignore wishlists from free users; every feature must help the customer reach the main result.
**Why:** Maurya (free users asked for features and never paid); Jobs (saying no); Hormozi (one thing).
**Dissent:** Bezos: dissatisfaction is the signal; ask what job the feature serves, then build the smallest thing that does it.
**Test:** For each request ask "would you pay for this, or pay more because of it?" Build only what gets a yes from payers.

**9. "Growth is flat / people sign up and disappear."**
**Call:** It's an activation problem before it's a marketing problem. Find the early action that predicts a retained user, then rebuild onboarding to get everyone to it fast. Do the first 50 follow-ups by hand.
**Why:** Zuckerberg (the "friends in the first days" insight); Maurya (happy-customer loop); Hormozi (the 4 Rs).
**Dissent:** Graham: if weekly growth is flat *and* retention is fine, you may just not be reaching people, so fix distribution.
**Test:** Define the activation event; measure % of signups reaching it within 24h. Pass = move it 20 points in two weeks. Then see `ai-era.md` (lifecycle engine).

**10. "Should I make a community / brand around this?"**
**Call:** If customers get value from *each other* (identity, status, shared outcomes), yes; community is a moat a feature can't copy. If the product is a solo utility, don't force it.
**Why:** Reilly (co-created brand, scarcity); Chesky; `ai-era.md` (CaaS).
**Test:** Do customers talk to each other unprompted? Seed a group with 20 users and watch for 2 weeks. Pass = 5+ organic peer-to-peer posts.

**11. "Should I spend on ads, PR or events?"**
**Call:** Not before pull exists. Ads amplify a converting offer; they don't create one. PR agencies and events are the classic waste.
**Why:** Seibel/Caldwell (waste money list); Hormozi (Core Four only after the offer converts); Zuckerberg (spend on the product and activation first).
**Test:** Run $200-500 of ads only if you already have a landing page converting at or above your threshold; otherwise fix the page. Pass = CAC below 1/3 of first-year gross profit.

**12. "My business depends on one platform (an API, an app store, one ad network)."**
**Call:** Own the customer relationship (email/SMS list, community) and a second channel before the platform changes terms. Zuckerberg's own scar: Facebook bet on the wrong mobile approach (HTML5) and had to course-correct hard when the platform shifted.
**Why:** Zuckerberg (platform paranoia); Bezos (what won't change); Buffett/Munger (moat).
**Test:** What share of revenue dies if that platform changes pricing or access tomorrow? Pass = under 50%, with an owned list ≥ 10% of customers.

**13. "Are we at product-market fit? Should we scale?"**
**Call:** Don't scale until customers are trying to rip it out of your hands: retention curve flattening, referrals unprompted, sales pulling. If you're guessing, you're not there.
**Why:** Andreessen (you can feel it); Graham (weekly growth); Maurya (activation/retention before acquisition).
**Test:** Three signals: ≥40% of users say they'd be very disappointed without it (a commonly used heuristic); retention curve flattens; ≥20% of new users come from referral. Two of three = go; otherwise iterate.

## Money & funding

**14. "How should I price?"**
**Call:** Pick a number tonight; you can change it later. Price off the value delta, not your cost; narrower niche = higher price; prepay beats per-use. Watch the three seconds after you say it.
**Why:** Seibel (3 seconds); Hormozi (specific = pricier: $19 → $199 → $10k as the niche narrows); Maurya.
**Test:** Quote the number to 10 buyers. Pass = fewer than 8 flinch hard; if nobody pushes back, you're too cheap, so raise it 2× for the next 10.

**15. "Free or paid? Should I do freemium?"**
**Call:** Paid from day one. Freemium only with a real upgrade constraint and tracked conversion.
**Why:** Maurya (nine months of free users that never paid); Seibel.
**Test:** Charge the first 10 users any amount. Pass = 3 pay. Free users' requests don't count.

**16. "Should I raise venture capital?"**
**Call:** Default no. Bootstrap to ~10 paying customers first. Raise only if the business is capital-heavy, winner-take-all and credibly 100×. Understand what you give up: dilution, terms and a clock, not just the valuation.
**Why:** Seibel/Caldwell; Maurya (customers validate your model, investors validate theirs); Graham (default alive); Rho mechanics in `monetization.md`.
**Dissent:** Altman/Thiel: if it's a hard-tech or network-effects business, capital *is* the strategy. Bootstrapping a robot company won't work.
**Test:** Write the 100× story in 3 sentences. If you can't, don't raise. Pass-to-raise = story holds *and* you have 10 paying customers or a prototype that earns serious intros.

**17. "An investor/friend wants to put money in. Take it?"**
**Call:** Valuation is the headline, terms decide who controls the company. Ask "what did I give up to get it?" Never take money you can't describe the strings on; never give equity for advice or PR.
**Test:** Can you explain liquidation preference, protective provisions, pro-rata and drag-along in plain words? If not, get a lawyer's one-hour review before signing.

**18. "Should I quit my job?"**
**Call:** The math: 3-6 months of personal savings plus side income that matches your salary for 3-6 months. But fear usually isn't about math; "play it out" the specific worst case (move home, lose savings, take a similar job later).
**Why:** Hormozi (guaranteed bad vs. a chance at good; fear lives in the vague); Maurya; Graham.
**Test:** Write the specific worst case and the step you'd take. If you can live with it and meet the math, go.

## Team, focus, control

**19. "Should I hire my first person?"**
**Call:** Pre-PMF, no: do it yourself. Exception: a role that directly makes revenue and that you can describe as a checklist (e.g. someone whose only job is to call every new lead within 60 seconds).
**Why:** Seibel & Caldwell (hiring mistakes); Hormozi (barbell, document-demonstrate-duplicate); Jobs (A players, once you can afford one).
**Test:** Ask "is that my job?" for every task. If you can't write the checklist, you're not ready to hire it.

**20. "Do I need a co-founder?"**
**Call:** Prefer 2-3 founders, but never add one to please investors, and date first: a 3-month side project to see how you handle conflict. Solo is viable; the psychological toll is underestimated.
**Why:** Altman (never add one for investors; keep looking); Maurya.
**Test:** Ship a small joint project in 90 days. Pass = you'd do another with them and conflict resolved in <48h.

**21. "Should I sell myself or hire a salesperson?"**
**Call:** Sell it yourself until you've documented what works (roughly the first ~$1M), then train others. If the model only works at your level of selling, the model is broken.
**Why:** Hormozi; Maurya.
**Test:** Hand a rep your script and watch 10 calls. Pass = they close at least half your rate.

**22. "Should I pivot?"**
**Call:** Diagnose first: fear (vague) or data (specific)? Count real iterations. Your hypothesis was probably wrong from the jump, so iterate on the *problem/wedge/price* you're learning about; pivot only on specific disproof. Beware the woman in the red dress: every new idea looks perfect because you can't see its flaws yet.
**Why:** Altman (100 bricks); Zuckerberg (hypothesis wrong from the jump, still the same company); Hormozi (don't restart the clock at year zero). → `coach.md`.
**Dissent:** Graham/Butterfield: sometimes the real product is inside the thing you built (Slack came from a game that failed); if one use of your tool is thriving and the rest isn't, follow the pull.
**Test:** 3 numbers: iterations done in the last 90 days, # of people pulling (asking for it), paid conversions. If iterations <10 and nobody's pulling, iterate; if 10+ and flat, change the wedge, not the whole company.

**23. "I want to start a second project while the first isn't working."**
**Call:** No. Compare year-four of your current thing against year-zero of the new one, not year-zero to year-zero. The grass is greener because you don't know what it's fertilized with.
**Why:** Hormozi (focus; FOMO; commitment); Altman.
**Test:** Write down what you'd do to double the current project in 30 days. If you can't think of anything, that's the problem to fix, not a reason to switch.

**24. "When do I delegate or let go of control?"**
**Call:** In order: support → billing/admin → selling → managing a sales team → leading leaders. Give up execution, keep visibility. Rule: if you can't name the flaws in a department, you're too far away.
**Why:** Hormozi (buyback ladder; where the bodies are buried; time audit); Rabois (barrels).
**Dissent:** Chesky/Jobs: don't delegate the product's soul.
**Test:** 7-day, 15-minute time log. Hire for the largest block that isn't product or selling.

**25. "Do I need a mentor/advisor?"**
**Call:** You need *models*, not mentors: study how the best decide and do 100 reps with feedback. Pay in money or time to compress years of mistakes; never pay advisors in equity; ask them to invest if they believe.
**Why:** Hormozi (models, not mentors); Seibel/Caldwell.
**Test:** After each advice session, name one decision you changed. None = stop meeting.

## Launch, brand, safety

**26. "When should I launch?"**
**Call:** Now, for anything reversible. Embarrassing v1 beats a perfect v6 that nobody asked for. Exceptions: the core path of trust-critical products (payments, health, email), and abuse/cost safety (rate limits, spend caps; see `ai-era.md`).
**Why:** Zuckerberg/Hoffman (ship); Seibel (launch to find out); Jobs (don't ship junk, ship *less*).
**Test:** Put it in front of 10 strangers this week. Pass = 3 reach the aha unaided.

**27. "How much should I invest in brand, logo and name?"**
**Call:** Almost nothing at first. A brand is a gut feeling created by behavior, product and support, not a logo. Duolingo, Facebook and Airbnb all started ugly. Spend the time on being polarizing and specific.
**Why:** Neumeier (brand = gut feeling); Wurwand/Hormozi (stand for something); Gohar Khan's student-founder rule: make it exist first, refine later.
**Test:** Ask 10 customers to describe you in one sentence. If it matches your one-liner, the brand is working.

**28. "What should I worry about security/cost abuse on my vibe-coded app?"**
**Call:** Protect every endpoint that costs you money (AI calls, search, uploads, email/SMS), not just login. Rate limits, quotas, throttles, CAPTCHA, spend caps. → `ai-era.md` §"Abuse & cost safety". This isn't premature scaling; it's cheap insurance.
**Test:** Try to call your costliest endpoint 1,000 times in a minute from one IP. Pass = blocked, alerted, and capped at the provider.

**29. "I'm a student / broke / have no skills or network."**
**Call:** You have more than you think: a problem you hit, a skill, a few hundred reachable people. Pick a pain/profession/passion, pick one narrow who ("people like you"), and sell an outcome by hand before any LLC or logo. A semi-private group service (1-to-8) can out-earn 1-to-1 by ~4× per hour with no capital.
**Why:** Hormozi (3 Ps, warm outreach, test prep); Gohar Khan (MVP, first 10 users, feedback patterns); Chesky.
**Test:** 50 DMs using "I made X that helps [who] do Y. Want to try it?" Pass = 3 trials, 1 paid.

**30. "I feel guilty / pressured / like I'm not normal for wanting this."**
**Call:** Fear and "shoulds" live in the vague; get specific. The courage to be yourself is also the strongest differentiator in a crowded market. Don't dilute yourself for the 20% who'll never like you.
**Why:** Hormozi (courage; play it out; unconditional shoulds).
**Test:** Write the specific worst case, the specific cost, and the specific next step. Do the step.

## Using this file
- If no entry fits, run the generic protocol in `council.md` and state which stage and goal you assumed.
- If two entries conflict for this founder, say so and rule on stage, goal, reversibility and evidence.
- Every call ends with a test with a pass/fail number and the next 48 hours. The numbers above are infounder's own starting thresholds, not source claims. Adjust to the business.
